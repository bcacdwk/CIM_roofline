\section{Workload Matching and Design Implications}
\label{sec:implications}

Fig.~\ref{fig:critical-reuse} compares six Qwen3.6-35B-A3B operating points from Table~\ref{tab:workload-intensity} with the native hardware balance intensities shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:hardware-capacities}(a). The $\SI$--$\SI^*$ comparison identifies the tighter service requirement for each stage and window. Increasing weight reuse can shift the bottleneck from resident updates to streaming calculation, while near-ridge matches remain sensitive to hardware conditions. These are reference comparisons; deployment requires aligned accounting for input sharing, replay and resident updates.

Technology selection must consider absolute throughput and storage density, rather than $\SI^*$ alone: a low $\SI^*$ may reflect slow streaming calculation rather than fast resident updates. For resident-bound work, faster updates (program/erase) or greater $\SI$ through fewer reloads raise the bound, whereas improving $\rho$ alone does not. For streaming-bound work, the priority is to reduce the delays limiting evaluation latency, such as ADC conversion time, adder-tree latency, or array/interconnect RC delay. Compact storage (3D stacking) can help sustain weight reuse by keeping more state resident, but the benefit depends on usable capacity and the weight eviction policy. Growing KV caches during attention acceleration also require an update organization suited to incremental writes.
