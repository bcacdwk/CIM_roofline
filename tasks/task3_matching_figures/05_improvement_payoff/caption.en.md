Analytic gains from improving one capability route at three native full-load reference states. The typical RRAM engine uses W[64,128] and U = 128; the typical MRAM engine uses W[32,256] at U = 128 and U = 1K. Each curve scales either the streaming capability rho (solid) or the resident-update capability tau (dashed) by f, holding the other fixed. Gain is min(f rho, tau U/N)/min(rho, tau U/N) or min(rho, f tau U/N)/min(rho, tau U/N), respectively. Filled and open medium symbols mark the two curves at f = 2; common axes preserve the relative gain scale across panels. Resident improvement at the RRAM state increases the resource upper bound until the 2.91-fold ceiling. MRAM at U = 128 is nearly balanced, so even unlimited improvement of its resident route yields only 1.04-fold gain; at U = 1K, streaming improvement can instead yield up to 7.71-fold gain. Increasing the currently non-limiting route alone leaves this two-route upper bound unchanged. These are analytical component-resource limits, not measured speedups, feasible simultaneous peaks, or equal-cost engineering alternatives. Each state serves U complete native input vectors per full resident load, including the first use; 1K = 1024.
